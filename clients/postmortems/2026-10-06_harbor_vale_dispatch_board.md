# Post-mortem - Harbor & Vale Logistics, dispatch board agent (Build phase)

Date: 2026-10-06 · Written by: the delivery manager · Client state: Active · Implementation: Dispatch board agent, Building -> Testing

## What happened

We scoped the dispatch board as a fixed-price build at 20% under our list rate to win the account.
Scope was "one board, one roster sheet". By week three, the client's three dispatch leads each
wanted their own view, and approvals for every change went through Dana plus two regional managers.
Each round of approval took four to six days. We absorbed 11 unbilled days.

## What we think we learned (drafts - not yet the agency's position)

- When a client's approval chain has more than two people in it, fixed-price scope does not hold.
  Price the approval chain, not the feature list.
- A discount offered to win a logo became the client's reference price: the first change request
  was priced against the discounted rate, not the list rate.
- "One board" meant one per lead to the client and one in total to us. Write the number of
  consumers into the scope line, not just the artifact.
- The weekly call slot (Tuesdays 10:00) kept the account healthy even while the build slipped;
  the health score never went below amber because contact never lapsed.

## What we would do next time

- Name every approver in the kickoff and put a two-business-day approval window in the SOW.
- Quote logo discounts as a one-time credit on the invoice, never as a lower rate.
- Convert fixed-price builds to time-and-materials the moment a second consumer appears.

## Open questions

- Do we keep taking fixed-price work from clients with committee approvals at all?
- Should the pilot offer (full rate, four weeks) be the only entry point for new logos?
